ThrowLab practice guide

How to record a disc golf throw for analysis

Record in landscape slow motion from the side, with the throwing hand and disc visible through release and early flight. ThrowLab recommends 240 fps. Trim the clip to include the complete throw, then choose a clear frame and center the disc in the finder.

Before you throw

  1. Choose the setup in ThrowLab. Select the disc, throwing style, and intended line so the result is evaluated against the right target.
  2. Position the camera to the side. Keep the release area and early flight in view. Place the camera outside the throwing path.
  3. Use landscape slow motion. Leave room for the full motion and the disc after release. Use good lighting so the disc edge remains visible.

What to keep in the clip

Include the full throw and several frames of the disc after it leaves your hand. You do not need to capture the landing. A practice net can work if the disc is visible for several frames before it reaches the net.

Find the disc before analyzing

Move to a clear early-flight frame, zoom as needed, and center the disc completely in the finder circle. If the disc is blurred, blocked, or too small to identify, try a clearer frame or record another throw.

If the result is unclear

Check lighting, motion blur, camera angle, and disc visibility first. A higher frame rate helps capture more of the release, but it does not correct poor framing or an obscured disc.

Video results are estimates. A cleaner recording improves the evidence available for analysis; it does not guarantee an exact measurement.
