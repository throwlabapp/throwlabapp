ThrowLab practice guide

How to track your disc golf practice progress

Compare throws made with the same disc, throwing style, and intended line. Look at speed, disc angle, and release-path target hits separately, then use repeated sessions to see whether those readings are becoming more consistent.

Keep the comparison useful

ThrowLab groups progress by disc, throwing style, and intended line. A Firebird thrown RHBH on a straight line belongs to a different comparison from the same disc thrown on hyzer. Keep your recording setup similar too, since camera changes can affect the readings.

Read each metric separately

Your disc angle may become more consistent while speed still varies. Reviewing the three metrics separately helps identify what is improving and what still needs attention.

Illustrative example, not a user result

If three of five clear disc-angle readings met the target earlier, and four of five met it later, the later group has more target matches. That is a useful signal to keep watching, not proof of lasting improvement from ten throws.

Look across sessions

Throws within the same practice window are grouped automatically. Review several comparable sessions rather than judging practice from your best throw. Small groups can vary, and unclear readings provide less evidence.

Use milestones as a running count

Milestones count dialed-in throws. That count records successful attempts over time, but it is not a success rate by itself. Read it alongside your recent target matches to understand consistency.

Choose one focus for the next session

Use your results to pick a specific adjustment, then repeat the same throw setup. Track whether the relevant metric meets its target more often while the others remain consistent.

Release target matches describe video-based readings. To follow distance separately, use Track distance to mark your throw and final disc positions with GPS. Compare qualifying distance averages for the same disc, style, and line; GPS estimates do not measure the curved flight path or scoring improvement.
