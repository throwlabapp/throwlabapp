ThrowLab practice guide

How to get more out of disc golf net practice

Record a throw into the net and check the flight readings after each set. A net can’t say how far a throw would have gone, so use it for how the disc leaves your hand and measure distance out on a field.

A net is where most of your throws happen over the winter, and most of those throws teach you nothing because nobody’s checking what they did. Recording turns each set into something you can compare.

Set up the camera

Put your phone to the side of the throw, in landscape, on something solid. Discs bounce back off nets, so keep the phone well away from where the disc will end up, and don’t stand it where a rebound can reach it. Slow motion needs good light, and a dim garage is the usual problem, so turn on every light you have. The disc has to stay in view for several frames before it hits the net, so back the camera up until the throwing area and the first stretch of flight are both in frame. The recording guide has the full setup.

Run a set

Pick the disc and your throwing style in ThrowLab before you start, so every throw saves to the right place. Throw a handful, and check speed, disc tilt, and launch angle after each one or after the set. You’re looking for the numbers that stay put and the numbers that jump around. A reading that holds steady is a habit, and a reading that jumps is a place to start working.

If you change your grip or release, change one thing and throw another set. A set with two changes mixed together doesn’t tell you which one did anything.

Where a net falls short

Every throw stops at the net, so the net can’t tell you how far a throw would have gone. Speed, disc tilt, and launch angle are fine to measure there. Distance isn’t, so go out to a field once in a while and use Measure Distance on real throws. Your net work and your field results are separate sets of numbers, and it helps to know which is which.

Tracking a winter of practice

Throws save to the disc and throwing style you chose, and they group into sessions automatically. After a few weeks, Trends shows how your speed, disc tilt, and launch angle have moved for each disc. Tracking your progress covers how to compare sessions.

Questions people ask

How many throws should I record?

Enough to see a pattern. A handful per disc per session is plenty. There’s no need to record every throw you make.

Can I record outdoors and upload later?

Yes. Upload any 240 fps slow-motion clip from your photo library.
