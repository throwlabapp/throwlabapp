ThrowLab practice guide

Disc golf throw analysis without a sensor disc

You can get speed, disc tilt, and launch angle from your phone. ThrowLab reads a slow-motion video of your throw, so nothing gets attached to a disc and nothing needs charging.

If you want to know how you’re throwing, there are two broad routes: put sensors in a disc, or point a camera at your throw. Both work. They suit different people, so here’s the honest difference.

What a sensor disc is good at

A sensor disc measures every throw as it happens, including in a net, and you never set up a camera. It can also measure things a video can’t see from outside the disc, like spin. If you want a measurement on every throw with no recording step, that convenience is real.

Where it limits you

The sensors live in one disc. That disc is the one you’re analyzing, and it’s not the drivers, fairways, mids, and putters you play on the course. A sensor disc also means buying hardware before you find out whether the data helps, and keeping it charged.

What ThrowLab does instead

ThrowLab asks you to record each throw or upload a clip, and gives you speed, disc tilt, and launch angle from the video. In return it works with any disc, exactly as it comes out of your bag. Nothing is added to the disc, so it feels and flies the same as always, and each disc keeps its own history. You can compare your driver with your fairway driver, or one plastic with another.

Distance is a separate measurement. You walk to your disc and ThrowLab gives you the distance with no GPS drift, which works under trees as well as on an open field.

Which one fits you

If you mostly throw one disc in a net and want a number on every throw without touching your phone, a sensor disc fits. If you want to analyze the discs you actually play, measure distance on the course, or find out what the numbers look like before paying for anything, a phone fits. The first 5 ThrowLab analyses are free, flight or distance, so you can try it on a few throws first.

Questions people ask

Do I need special equipment?

Only an iPhone that records 240 fps slow motion, running iOS 17 or later.

Can I upload a video I already recorded?

Yes. Record in the app or upload a slow-motion clip from your photo library.

Does ThrowLab measure spin?

No. ThrowLab reads speed, disc tilt, and launch angle from video, and measures distance by walking to your disc.
